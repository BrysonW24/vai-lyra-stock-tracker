Per-tier evaluation — how it works, and what it produces

Every stock is judged against its own weight class: a small cap doubling is graded against how often small caps double — and against a dumb "just pick the jumpiest small caps" baseline. The output is one verdict grid that says where the model genuinely knows something.

The pipeline

Runs inside the existing standing loop (purged walk-forward · one-shot holdout · clustered CIs)

1 · Tag Tier at prediction date Micro <$300M · Small $300M–2B · Mid $2–10B · Large >$10B — cap as it was then, never today's.
2 · Chance Per-tier base rates How often does each tier hit +X% anyway, per quarter? This table is "random pick" for that group.
3 · Baselines Two dumb models, per tier Random-within-tier (floor) and jumpiest-within-tier (the real bar — volatility alone predicts barrier touches).
4 · Model Score within tier Top 5% of micros vs micros, top 5% of smalls vs smalls. Never pooled — a pooled top list is all micros again.
5 · Compare Model vs its tier's baselines Paired, symbol-clustered bootstrap on the difference; permutation band across the threshold sweep; positives counted per cell.
6 · Verdict The grid → product gate A tier is claimed only where it beats its own jumpiness baseline with CI separation. Everything else greys out.

Standing rule: the product only surfaces a tier × threshold cell inside the claimed outline. Grey cells render greyed in the UI too — "no demonstrated skill here" is shown, not hidden.

Numbers in the mock follow the pre-registered prediction (skill peaks in micro/small at moderate-to-+100% moves; +10% is a market-direction question; +200/300% and most of mid/large are too thin to claim). n = winners in the holdout for that cell — the honesty number: where it is tiny, no score is shown.
